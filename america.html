U.S. Visa and Electronic Travel Authorization (ESTA) Information

Travelers to the United States must obtain either a visa or an electronic travel authorization (ESTA) before departure, depending on their nationality, purpose of travel, and length of stay. Even for tourism or short-term business trips, entry without completing the required procedures in advance is generally not permitted. Because the requirements vary depending on individual circumstances, it is important to confirm which procedure applies to you at the planning stage of your trip.

Visas vs. Electronic Travel Authorization

Whether a visa or an electronic travel authorization (ESTA) is right for your trip to the United States depends mainly on the following factors:

  • Whether you are traveling for tourism or short-term business, or for work, study, or a long-term stay
  • How long you plan to stay
  • Whether your nationality is eligible for ESTA under the Visa Waiver Program

If you qualify for ESTA for a short stay, you may be able to use the electronic travel authorization; otherwise, you will need to obtain a visa appropriate to your purpose of travel.

Visas

The United States offers many types of visas depending on the purpose of travel, including work, study, family stays, and long-term residence. Application requirements, supporting documents, processing times, and permitted lengths of stay vary significantly by visa type. Even for tourism, a visa may be required depending on the length and conditions of your stay. It is important to choose the visa that matches your purpose of travel and allow ample time to prepare.

The Electronic Travel Authorization: ESTA

ESTA is a program for travelers from countries and regions participating in the Visa Waiver Program who are visiting the United States for a short period for purposes such as tourism or short-term business. ESTA is a pre-travel authorization; once approved, it is electronically linked to your passport information. Applications must be submitted through the official U.S. government website or official app, and an approved ESTA does not automatically guarantee entry into the United States.

About U.S. Visas

The United States issues many types of visas, with different requirements depending on the purpose of travel and conditions of stay. Below is an overview of some of the most common visa types.

Visitor Visa (B-2)

This visa is used for tourism, vacations, visiting family and friends, and short-term medical treatment. The permitted length of stay is determined at the port of entry and may be up to approximately six months. Employment and other paid activities are generally not permitted.

Student Visa (F-1)

This visa is for full-time study at U.S. universities, language schools, vocational schools, and other institutions. The period of stay corresponds to the duration of study, and holders may remain in the country lawfully while enrolled. On-campus employment and similar work may be permitted only under certain conditions.

Business Visa (B-1)

This visa is used for short-term, unpaid business activities such as attending meetings and negotiations, site visits, and contract discussions. The permitted length of stay is determined at the port of entry, generally ranging from a few weeks up to about six months.

Work Visa (H-1B)

This visa is for employment with a U.S. employer in a specialty occupation requiring specialized knowledge or skills. It must be petitioned for and approved by the employer, and the period of stay is generally up to three years, extendable to a maximum of six years.

Important Notes on U.S. Visas

The U.S. visa system is highly detailed: even within the same purpose category, there are multiple classifications, including temporary, long-term, and immigrant visas. Application requirements, permitted lengths of stay, work authorization, and renewability also vary considerably from visa to visa.

Because the rules are subject to change, always check the latest official information before applying. Note that even with an approved ESTA or visa, the final decision on entry is made at the port of entry by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP).

About ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorization)

ESTA (Electronic System for Travel Authorization) is a pre-travel authorization program for travelers from countries and regions participating in the Visa Waiver Program (VWP). While ESTA is not a visa, it serves in place of one for short-term travel for tourism or business purposes.

Once approved, your ESTA is electronically linked to your passport and is verified by airlines and at immigration. Eligibility for ESTA depends on your nationality and other conditions — not all travelers qualify.

What ESTA Does and Does Not Allow

The main purposes of travel permitted under ESTA are tourism, vacations, visiting friends and family, and short-term business activities (such as attending meetings, negotiations, and trade shows, provided no remuneration is involved).

The following activities are not permitted under ESTA:

  • Employment in the United States (whether paid or unpaid)
  • Long-term study or degree programs
  • Internships or training programs that are considered employment
  • Paid entertainment or athletic performances
  • Travel for the purpose of permanent or long-term residence
  • Long-term stays for medical treatment

Applying for ESTA: Validity and Key Points

ESTA applications are submitted through the official U.S. government website or official app. You will need a valid passport, personal information, and answers to travel-related eligibility questions, which are then screened.

ESTA is valid for a set period, during which multiple short visits are allowed; however, each stay is generally limited to a maximum of 90 days.

An approved ESTA does not guarantee entry into the United States. The final decision is made upon arrival by U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP).
