How to apply on the official site

The details differ by country, but the process on every official government site follows the same four steps.

1

Check your eligibility

Confirm on the official site that your nationality, purpose of travel and length of stay qualify for the electronic authorization rather than a visa.

2

Prepare passport & card

Have a valid passport (usually an e-passport), a credit or debit card for the official fee, and an email address you check regularly.

3

Apply on the official site

Use only the government site or its official app linked on this page. Enter your details exactly as they appear in your passport and pay the official fee.

4

Keep the approval email

Wait for the approval email before you travel and keep it with your reference number. Most authorizations are linked electronically to your passport.

Beware of unofficial websites charging extra fees

Many websites imitate government services and charge far more than the official fee, sometimes without submitting anything. The official fees are listed above and are the only amounts the governments charge. Check that the address ends in the official government domain (for example .gov, .go.kr, .gov.uk, .europa.eu, .gov.au, .govt.nz, .canada.ca, .go.jp) before entering any personal or payment details.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a government website? +

No. Global Travel Center is an independent information guide operated by a private company in Japan. We have no connection with any government. Only the government bodies themselves issue travel authorizations and visas, and all official application sites are linked on this page.

Do you accept or process applications? +

No. We do not accept, prepare, submit or review applications, and we do not collect any fees. Apply directly on the official government site for your destination — that is the only place your application is processed, and you pay only the official fee.

What is the difference between a visa and an electronic travel authorization (ETA)? +

A visa is a formal permission, usually issued by an embassy or consulate, often requiring documents and sometimes an interview. An electronic travel authorization (ESTA, K-ETA, UK ETA, ETIAS, NZeTA, eTA and similar) is a lighter pre-travel screening for nationals who do not need a visa for short visits: you apply online, pay a small fee and are usually approved within minutes to a few days. An ETA does not guarantee entry — the final decision is always made by the border officer on arrival.

How long before travel should I apply? +

As soon as you start planning, and before you buy non-refundable tickets. Official guidance is to apply at least 72 hours before departure for ESTA, to allow 72 hours for K-ETA and NZeTA, and to allow up to 3 working days for UK ETA. Applications that need extra checks can take longer (for example up to 30 days for ETIAS if an interview is required).

What if my application is refused? +

A refusal of an electronic travel authorization does not stop you from travelling — it usually means you must apply for a regular visa at the embassy or consulate of that country instead. First check whether the refusal was caused by a simple data error (a mistyped passport number can often be corrected by re-applying where the official site allows it). Official fees are generally non-refundable, and reasons for refusal are given only by the government concerned.

Is K-ETA currently required for Japanese nationals? +

No, not at the moment. According to the official K-ETA notice of 23 December 2025, the temporary exemption for the currently exempt countries and regions — including Japan — has been extended from 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026 (KST). Japanese nationals can enter South Korea without a K-ETA during this period. Travellers who want the benefits of an approved K-ETA (such as not filling in an arrival card) may still apply voluntarily; the fee is then charged. Check the official site for the rules after 31 December 2026.
