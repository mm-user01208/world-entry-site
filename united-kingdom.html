United Kingdom Visa and Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) Information

Travellers to the United Kingdom must obtain either a visa or an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) in advance, depending on their nationality, purpose of travel, and length of stay. Even for tourism or short-term business trips, entry without completing the required procedures in advance is generally not permitted. Because the applicable scheme varies depending on individual circumstances, it is important to confirm the necessary procedures at the planning stage of your trip.

Visas vs. Electronic Travel Authorisation

Whether a visa or an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) is right for your trip to the UK depends mainly on the following factors:

  • Whether you are travelling for tourism or short-term business, or for work, study, or a long-term stay
  • How long you plan to stay
  • Whether your nationality is eligible for the ETA (visa-exemption) scheme

Visas

The UK offers a range of visas depending on the purpose of travel, including work, study, family stays, and long-term residence. Application requirements, supporting documents, processing times, and permitted lengths of stay vary significantly by visa type.

The Electronic Travel Authorisation: ETA

The Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) is a pre-travel authorisation scheme for travellers from certain countries and regions visiting the UK for short stays, such as for tourism or short-term business. An ETA is not a visa, but it is required for those travelling to the UK visa-free.

About UK Visas

The UK issues many types of visas, with different requirements depending on the purpose of travel and conditions of stay. Below is an overview of the most common visa types.

Standard Visitor Visa

This visa is used for tourism, holidays, visiting family and friends, short-term medical treatment, and unpaid business activities. Stays are generally permitted for up to six months.

Student Visa

This visa is for full-time study at UK educational institutions such as universities, graduate schools, and language schools. Limited work may be permitted only under certain conditions.

Skilled Worker Visa

This visa is for employment with a UK employer in roles requiring particular skills or qualifications. Sponsorship from the employer is required.

Family Visa

This visa is for living with family members residing in the UK, such as a spouse, partner, parent, or child. Strict requirements apply.

Important Notes on UK Visas

The UK visa system is relatively segmented: even within the same purpose category, there are multiple classifications, such as short-stay, long-stay, and work routes. The rules are subject to change, so always check the latest official information before applying. Note that even with an ETA or visa, the final decision on entry is made at the border by the UK Home Office and the immigration authorities.

About the Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA)

The Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) is a pre-travel authorisation scheme for travellers from visa-exempt countries and regions visiting the UK. While an ETA is not a visa, it takes the place of one for short stays for tourism or business purposes.

What the ETA Does and Does Not Allow

The main purposes of travel permitted under the ETA are tourism, holidays, visiting friends and family, and short-term business activities (such as attending meetings, negotiations, and trade shows, provided no remuneration is involved).

The following activities are not permitted under the ETA:

  • Employment in the UK (whether paid or unpaid)
  • Long-term study or degree programmes
  • Internships or training programmes that are considered employment
  • Paid entertainment or sporting activities
  • Travel for the purpose of permanent or long-term residence
  • Long-term stays for medical treatment

Applying for the ETA: Validity and Key Points

ETA applications are made through the official UK government website or the official app. An ETA is valid for a set period, during which multiple short visits are possible; however, each stay must remain within the limits of a short visit.

An approved ETA does not guarantee entry into the UK. The final decision is made upon arrival by the immigration authorities.
